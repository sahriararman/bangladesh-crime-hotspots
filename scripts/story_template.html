The pattern

One-third of the reports, 3% of the map

Statistical testing finds {{hot}} hotspots. Together they hold {{inside_pct}}% of the incidents in about {{area_pct}}% of the mapped area.

These are reports, so they show where crime gets recorded. That may not be the same as where it happens most.

What's being reported

Almost half involve a death

Bodies found ({{top_mix_pct}}%) and murders ({{mix2_pct}}%) make up the two largest groups in the records. The two can overlap in real life: a body found today may later become a murder case.

Greater Dhaka

{{dhaka_n}} incidents in one connected area

The largest hotspot takes in Dhaka, Narayanganj and Gazipur. It holds {{dhaka_pct}}% of all the incidents in the file.

Inside the capital

Savar stands out on the edge of the city

Savar alone has {{savar_n}} incidents, more than any named Dhaka neighbourhood.

Don't read street-level detail here. Dots mark the centre of a named place, and {{city_n}} incidents nationwide have only a city-level location. Savar is a whole upazila, while Dhaka is split into many small neighbourhoods, which also makes Savar look larger.

Chattogram

The only other hotspot that never moves

{{ctg_n}} incidents fall in this area, {{ctg_city_n}} of them recorded simply as Chattogram city. Like Greater Dhaka, it stays significant at every search radius we tested.

The regions

Smaller signals, less certain

With the two giants set aside, Jashore ({{jashore_n}}) and Mymensingh ({{mym_n}}) appear at the 20 km radius.

Size changes the picture

Per person, the map looks different

Big cities have more reports partly because they have more people. Per 100,000 residents, {{top_rate_name}} ({{top_rate}}) is far above Dhaka metro ({{dhaka_rate}}) and Chattogram metro ({{ctg_rate}}).

Dots now show the rate for each area with at least 15 incidents. Larger and brighter means a higher rate.

The rankings

Regional centres top the list

Rates rise and fall easily in places with few residents, so a handful of cases can move an area up this list. Treat the order as approximate.

Density

Crowded doesn't mean riskier

Across {{units}} areas, population density tells you almost nothing about the rate of reported incidents. The correlation is {{rho}}, very close to zero.

Crime by crime

Not every crime is a Dhaka crime

Dhaka district holds {{dhaka_all}}% of all incidents, but that share changes a lot by crime. Nearly one robbery in three is reported there, compared with about one murder in nine.

Six crimes, six maps

Robbery crowds into the capital. Murder spreads out.

Greater Dhaka holds {{rob_pct}}% of robberies but only {{mur_pct}}% of murders. Mapping each crime on its own shows patterns that the combined map hides.

Regional fingerprints

Each division reports a different mix of crime

This chart ignores how many incidents a division has and asks what kind they are. In Mymensingh, murder's share of reports is {{mym_mur}} times the national share. In Dhaka, robbery's share is {{dh_rob_r}} times, and in Barisal, assault's is {{bar_ass}} times.

Some crimes are rarer than expected too: bodies found in Sylhet ({{syl_body}} times) and murder in Rangpur ({{ran_mur}} times).

The clock

Night is when most violence is reported

{{night_pct}}% of all incidents are recorded at night, and about two-thirds of murders, robberies and rapes.

Bodies found follow a different clock, with {{body_morning}}% recorded in the morning. That's probably when they're discovered, not when the deaths happened.

The calendar

An unexplained dip at the end of the year

November and December average {{late_avg}} incidents, about {{late_drop}}% below the {{other_avg}} average of other months. This might be a real seasonal drop, or the records for those months might be incomplete. The file has no year, so we can't tell which.

About the data

How a row became a dot

The source is a single file, Bangladesh-Crime-Dataset.csv. It doesn't state its source or the years it covers.

  1. {{raw}}rows in the fileEach lists a crime, place, time of day and local statistics
  2. {{n}}incidents kept{{dupes}} exact duplicates removed; some could be separate victims
  3. {{moved}}rows movedTheir coordinates were wrong, such as upazilas stacked on one city point. New positions are approximate
  4. {{sites}}locationsEach is the centre of a named place, not an exact address
  5. {{hot}}hotspotsGetis-Ord Gi* on {{cells}} grid cells of 10 km, 20 km radius, 5% false discovery rate
  • No trendsWithout years, the story can't say whether crime is rising or falling.
  • Reports, not all crimePlaces with more reporting or press coverage can look worse, and many crimes are never reported.
  • Population figuresRates use the population column in the file, and its census year isn't stated.
  • Approximate edgesHotspot outlines are smoothed for reading, and crime counts are too uneven for exact confidence levels.
  • What we left outWeather and literacy are in the file. Without figures for days with no incident, or a clear link, charts of them would mislead.
  • Totals onlyThis story shows counts for places. It identifies no victims or individual cases.
